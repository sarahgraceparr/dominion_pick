# Funmaxer report: k1, k2, k3

I watched all six logs: k1-2p, k1-4p, k2-2p, k2-4p, k3-2p and k3-4p. The players were scripted bots, so I scored what each kingdom made possible. I did not count bot mistakes against the kingdom, but I do flag them where they matter.

**What the scorer predicted and what happened.** The scorer ranked the kingdoms **k1 (102) > k3 (60) > k2 (29)**. By fun they finished **k2 > k1 > k3**. The scorer put the most-fun kingdom last and over-rated the least-fun one.

---

## 1. Game-by-game

### k1-2p: Peril combo, 2 players. Fun 62/100

**Best moments and big moves**

- **T2–T3, both players.** Peril turns a starting Necropolis into a $7 Loot (P1 got Jewels on T2, P2 got Prize Goat on T3). Both players get a "free treasure lottery" from turn 2, which is a good early hook.
- **T5 P1: three Perils in one turn.** Fortress went to the trash and came back three times, gaining Spell Scroll, Amphora and Insignia. The loop fired exactly as the scorer described.
- **T9 P1 → P2: a surprise interaction.** P1's Peril trashed Fortress, Market Square reacted for a Gold, and that Gold set off **P2's** Cutthroat, which handed P2 a Sword. P1's combo payoff paid the opponent. This is good, memorable interaction.
- **T10 P2: $19, 3 buys, 2 Provinces.** Pendant gave +$7 (on T7 it had given +$5). This was the biggest turn of the game.
- **T10 P1: $12 with 4 buys** (Orb, Spell Scroll and Gold, then a Province and a Rope). The Loots made every hand different.

**Low points**

- The loop needs **$2 and a Buy per use**, and there is only **one Fortress**. It fired on T3, T5, T7 and T9 (every other turn) and never turned into a scoring explosion. P1 had 0 VP until T10.
- The score was decided by T10 (P2 24–6). P1's closing run (T12 and T13 Provinces) never threatened.
- **Tools, Sage and Feodum were never bought.** Maroon was bought on T12 and never played. Three of the ten cards were dead.
- Cutthroat and Sword cut P1 to 3 cards right before Peril turns (T6, T9). That was mildly frustrating.
- The game was short: 13 turns, so there was little room for a comeback.

**Verdict.** This was a lively, texture-rich game. Loots are fun because every one is a different one-shot effect, and both players got a $12+ turn. But the advertised "combo" turned out to be a _shared_ engine of Loot gains, not a build-around that pays off. Ordinary Big Money with Pendant and a Cutthroat beat it comfortably because the loop is throttled by coins and buys. The kingdom has fun pieces, but it is narrower than its 10-card list suggests: 3–4 cards were irrelevant, and the 187-weight loop is really "+1 $7 Loot per $2 and Buy".

### k1-4p: Peril combo, 4 players. Fun 58/100

**Best moments and big moves**

- **T1–T3: all four players use Peril on Necropolis.** Sextant, Orb, Puzzle Box and Jewels arrive by turn 3. The shared "free Loot" hook works at every seat.
- **T5 P1: Peril on Fortress gives Doubloons, which gains a Gold.** It is a cute Loot-into-Gold moment.
- **T8–T9: Cutthroat paying out off other players' gains.** P3 got an Endless Chalice off P1's Gold. P2 got a Spell Scroll off P1's Peril Loot. This is the kingdom's signature interaction, and at 4p it fires constantly.
- **T9 and T10 P1: Peril twice in each turn,** for Puzzle Box + Insignia, then Hammer + Sword. The loop works, but modestly.
- **T13 P1: $12 for Province + Fortress. T15 P3: $11 with 4 buys for Province + Estate.** T15–T16: P1 takes the last two Provinces to break a three-way tie (P1 27, P2 27, P4 27 after T15).

**Low points**

- **No turn reached big-turn size:** there was never a 2-Province turn, never 3+ Loots in a turn, and nothing at $16+.
- **Market Square's Gold reaction never fired.** P1 Perilled a Fortress only 6 times, never more than twice in a turn, because +Buy was thin.
- **Cutthroat cut three hands to 3 cards on each of its 5 plays.** T8 P2 had $2 and bought nothing, and T11 P3 had $1. In a 4-player game this means a lot of small hands.
- **Sage, Maroon, Tools and Pendant were never bought.**
- P3, the Cutthroat rush, finished far behind on 16.

**Verdict.** This was a close, interactive, fast game (about 16 rounds). Four different plans ran (Fortress/Peril Loots, Cutthroat money, Cutthroat rush, and Feodum + Silver), and three of them finished within 6 VP. **Feodum was viable at 4p** (4 Feodums at 2 VP each, tied for second) when it had been absent in 2p. On the other hand, it had no fireworks. The combo was again just "Loot money", the payoff reaction never triggered, and four cards sat unused. It was pleasant and competitive, but flat at the top end.

### k2-2p: Repetition, 2 players. Fun 72/100

**Best moments and big moves**

- **T8 → T8+: the first Outpost pair.** Pearl Diver put a Gold on top, Treasury topdecked itself, and Caravan's next-turn card arrived in the extra turn. The result was the **first Province of the game**, bought in an extra turn.
- **T12+ → T13: the Treasury loop.** The 3-card extra-turn hand chained 2 Treasuries into a 3rd, all three were topdecked, and T13 _started_ with three Treasuries for $10 and a Province. A loop firing repeatedly is exactly what feels great.
- **T15 P1: $10 Province** from two topdecked Treasuries and two Golds.
- **T17 → T17+: +9 VP across an Outpost pair** (Duchy, then Province) with a Militia hit back at P2.
- **T19: the last Province decided a close race** (40–33). P2 was buying Duchies on T18 and hoping for a miss, and the outcome was live until the final buy.

**Low points**

- T2 ($2 with 3 Estates), **T10** (P2's Militia left a 3-card hand, $2, nothing bought), T16+ (three Coppers) and **T18+** ($1, Estate-clogged extra turn). Outpost's 3-card hands plus Militia make for occasional duds.
- **Pirate Ship, Workshop, Explorer, Harbinger and Festival were never bought.** Half the kingdom was unused.
- Interaction was only Militia (5 hits on P1).

**Verdict.** This was the most _satisfying_ game of the five. A real combo (Treasury topdecks into Outpost extra turns, with Caravan cashing in on the extra turn) came together, fired repeatedly and won a close game against honest Big Money. Big Money stayed competitive and never had a dead turn. The race swung (P2 led 9–9 then 15–15 and pulled level again around T16), and the final Province decided it. The narrow card usage and a few dead extra turns keep it out of the 80s.

### k2-4p: Repetition, 4 players. Fun 63/100

**Best moments and big moves**

- **T9 and T10 P1: back-to-back $8 Provinces** from the Caravan and Treasury chain.
- **T9–T10 P3: the Pirate Ship raids.** T9 trashed a Gold (P4), a Silver (P1) and a Silver (P2). T10 was Festival, Militia and Pirate Ship together: everyone was cut to 3 and P2 lost another Gold. In 4p, Pirate Ship is a real, dramatic attack: 11 treasures trashed in total.
- **P3's comeback arc.** P3 sat on 3 VP until T15, then the 4-token Pirate Ship (+$4) gave a Province on T15 and T17, reaching 23 VP. This was a strategy the scorer said was not viable.
- **T15 and T18 P1: 5-action chains** (2 Caravans, 2 Treasuries, Outpost) for $10 Provinces. On T18 an extra-turn Duchy followed, for +9 VP in one round.
- **T20 P4: double Caravan, $9**, for the Collector's only Province.

**Low points**

- **A runaway leader:** P1 finished on 50 against 27, 23 and 22, and had 15–21 VP from T10 while the rest were stuck on 9 or less.
- **Militia saturation:** 10 plays and 37 discard hits. P1's dead turns were T11+ ($1), T15+ ($2), T16 ($0 after Militia) and T20 ($2). P3 had dead turns on T1 and T9.
- Pirate Ship gutted P2's Big Money: only two $8+ hands all game.
- Harbinger and Explorer were never bought. Workshop only made P4's deck fat and slow.

**Verdict.** There was more variety and more interaction than in 2p. All four bots ran a different plan and all four bought Provinces, and Pirate Ship and Militia made the middle game loud. But the combo player ran away from a three-way scrum, and stacked Militia plus Outpost's small hands produced a lot of $0–2 turns. The game was fun to watch, less fun to be P2 in.

### k3-2p: Size Distortion, 2 players. Fun 34/100

**Best moments and big moves**

- **T11 P1: +10 VP on $2.** The deck crossed 30 cards, so every Gardens jumped to 3 VP.
- **T16 P1: +9 VP on $1.** The deck crossed 41 cards, so each Gardens was worth 4.
- **T15–T17 P2: three Provinces in a row** ($11, $10, $8). These were real money turns, but too late.
- **T17–T18 P1: a 3-pile ending.** P1 ended the game deliberately by gaining Workshops while 4 Provinces were still in the supply. This was a clever finish, but a cold one.

**Low points**

- **One-sided rush.** P1 had all 8 Gardens by T12 and every Estate by T16, and P2 never contested them. P1 led from T6 onward.
- **P1's turns were $1–2 with one Workshop play** from T9 to the end: lots of Workshops in hand, one played. That is low agency even though every turn "gained something".
- **Seven of ten kingdom cards were never bought:** Artisan, Bandit, Bureaucrat, Chapel, Festival, Sentry and Throne Room.
- Witch landed only twice (T9, T15), and on T15 one Witch drew the other, so the second Witch was dead.
- The game was over before any engine could exist.

**Verdict.** This was a narrow, grindy race of Gardens against money. One player shovelled Coppers and Estates and the other did not interact. The VP jumps at the Gardens thresholds are a fun "number goes up" moment, but nothing either player did changed the other's plan, and the result was obvious from about T11.

### k3-4p: Size Distortion, 4 players. Fun 46/100

**Best moments and big moves**

- **T9: a Witch storm.** P2 and P3 both played Witch, and with P4's Bureaucrat everyone was cursed or topdecked in one round. This was a high-interaction middle game.
- **T12 P3: $13 Province** off Sentry (which trashed a Curse and topdecked Gold) and Witch.
- **T14 P1: +13 VP** (Gardens at 4 each) and **T17 P1: +12 VP** (Gardens at 5 each). These were the biggest single-turn swings of all five games.
- **Surprise: the Witches fed the Gardens player.** Ten of P1's 58 cards were Curses that opponents had handed over, and they pushed P1 across the Gardens thresholds. The attack helped its target.
- **T20 P3 declined the last Province** with $11 (taking it would have lost) and bought a Duchy instead. It was a tense moment, but it was also the start of a stall.

**Low points**

- **P1 ran away with it:** 18 VP by T10 against 7 or less for everyone else, and 52 against 26–36 at the end.
- **P1 had $0–2 on 17 of 23 turns,** 5 terminal collisions (Workshop stuck in hand), and 4 dead turns (T18–T22: Coppers and Estates).
- **A five-round stall (T18–T23):** the last Province sat untouchable because taking it would lose, and the game crawled to a 3-pile ending on Estates, Curses and Gardens.
- **The engine never formed.** P3 never bought Festival or Throne Room, and Artisan, Bandit and Throne Room were never bought by anyone. The "Engine" and "Trash & payload" strategies that the scorer counted did not appear.
- Bureaucrat shrank hands to 4 cards on T4, T10, T17 and T20.

**Verdict.** There was more drama than in 2p: lots of attacks, three big Gardens jumps and a $13 turn. But the shape is the same: an uncontested Gardens rush that the other players' own attacks feed, a runaway leader, and a long, flat endgame where nobody wants to end the game. This was a mid-game party and an endgame chore.

## 2. Kingdom by kingdom

### k1: Peril combo. Static 102, Fun 60 (62 in 2p, 58 in 4p). **Overrated.**

- **Across player counts:**
  - **2p** had the bigger moments (a 3-Peril turn on T5, and $19 for 2 Provinces on T10) but was a lopsided money win.
  - **4p** was closer and broader. Four plans ran, a three-way tie stood at 27 after T15, and Feodum became viable. But there was no big turn, and Cutthroat's 3-card hits became routine.
  - In both games, the Fortress loop topped out at 2–3 Perils per turn, and 3–4 kingdom cards went unbought. Tools and Sage were never bought in either game, Maroon was never played, and Pendant was strong in 2p but unbought in 4p.
- **What the scorer got right:** Peril really is the centre of the kingdom (10 uses in the game). The Fortress + Peril loop works rules-wise. Market Square does react for a Gold. Shelters + Peril gives an early Loot.
- **What it OVERRATED:**
  - **The 187-weight loop.** It is a long chain of pairwise steps (20 "steps"), but every iteration costs $2 and a Buy and produces one Loot. It is a resource converter, not an engine. It fired 9 times in 2p and 6 times in 4p, and never more than 3 times in a turn. The Market Square Gold step fired once in 2p and never in 4p.
  - **"6 viable strategies" (+25).** Tools and Sage were never touched in either game, and "Variety" never appeared. Alt-VP (Feodum) did appear, but only in 4p.
  - **Synergies +35.** Many of the listed steps were things like Pendant rewarding Squire's Silvers, Rope trashing Feodum, or Maroon trashing Squire, and none of them happened.
- **What it UNDERRATED:**
  - **Peril is a _shared_ power.** Every player can use it on Necropolis, so it boosts Big Money as much as the combo.
  - **Pendant as a big-turn payoff** (+$5 and +$7).
  - **Cutthroat's cross-player Loot trigger,** which turned the combo player's Market Square Gold into a Sword for the opponent.
- **What surprised it:** the "combo" lost to money in 2p and won narrowly in 4p, both times as Loot money rather than an engine. Feodum + Squire + Tools became a competitive plan in 4p, where piles are bigger. The most interesting interaction was opponent-to-opponent (Cutthroat feeding off someone else's Gold gain), which the scorer did not model.

### k2: Repetition. Static 29, Fun 68 (72 in 2p, 63 in 4p). **Strongly underrated.**

- **Across player counts:** 2p was a close combo-against-money duel decided by the last Province. 4p added Pirate Ship (11 treasures trashed), saturating Militia and a real 4-way strategy split, but also a runaway combo leader and more dead small-hand turns.
- **What the scorer got right:** it found the exact winning synergy (Treasury + Outpost + Caravan). It also noted that two attacks hurt a little, which was true at 4p.
- **What it UNDERRATED:**
  - **"0 viable strategies" (−8) was simply wrong.** The scorer listed a combo synergy but did not count it as a strategy. The combo won both games.
  - **Pearl Diver as combo support,** topdecking Gold or Treasury into the Outpost hand on T8 and T14 in 2p, and T18 in 4p.
  - **Pirate Ship at 4 players.** It produced a viable aggressor plan and a comeback arc (P3 from 3 to 23 VP).
  - **Extra turns as a big-move generator.** 8 extra turns in 2p and 6 in 4p, with Provinces bought inside them.
- **What it OVERRATED:** almost nothing. "Villages available +5" (Festival) was irrelevant because nobody needed a village.
- **What surprised it:** self-topdecking + extra turn + duration made repeatable loop turns from 5-cost cards with no draw engine at all. The combo stayed competitive even through 5 Militia hits.

### k3: Size Distortion. Static 60, Fun 40 (34 in 2p, 46 in 4p). **Overrated.**

- **Across player counts:** 2p was a dull, uncontested rush. 4p was busier and swingier (Witch wars, +12 and +13 VP turns) but more lopsided, with a 5-round end stall. In both games the Gardens/Workshop player won comfortably.
- **What the scorer got right:** it flagged Gardens + Workshop as a synergy and penalised too many attacks and terminals. Terminal collision really did happen: 5 times for P1 in 4p, plus Witch drawing Witch.
- **What it OVERRATED:**
  - **"4 viable strategies" (+20).** Neither Engine nor Trash & payload ever materialised. The only draw is Witch (terminal +2) and Sentry (+1 card), and the villages that existed (Festival, Throne Room) never got bought.
  - **The Throne Room + Artisan and Throne Room + Bandit synergies.** Those cards were never bought.
  - **"Festival chains Witches".** Nobody did it.
  - **"Villages available +5".** The Gardens player could never reach $5 for Festival.
- **What it UNDERRATED:**
  - **How _dominant_ and _one-sided_ Gardens + Workshop is.** Its weight of 4 should be a warning, not a bonus.
  - **Cursers feeding Gardens.** The scorer listed "Chapel cleans Witch junk", but the real interaction was Witch _helping_ the Gardens victim.
- **What surprised it:** the attacks backfired. Ten Curses went into P1's Gardens deck, and Bureaucrat topdecked Estates onto P1, which P1 had no use for anyway. The endgame stalled because the money players could not afford to end the game.

## 3. Suggestions for the static scorer (ranked)

1. **Count a detected combo or loop synergy as a viable strategy (raise).**
   - **Rule:** if any synergy chain has weight ≥ 5 or `loop: true`, add "Combo" to `staticStrategies`, and never apply the "0 viable strategies" penalty.
   - **Keys on:** the synergy detector's output.
   - **Evidence:** k2 got −8 for "0 strategies" while listing Treasury + Outpost + Caravan. That combo won both k2 games and produced the best game of the set.

2. **Self-topdecking + extra turn (or a reduced next hand) is a big-move engine (raise).**
   - **Rule:** extra-turn cards ("Take an extra turn") combined with cards that return themselves or others to the deck top ("put this on top of your deck", "put it onto your deck", Pearl Diver/Harbinger-style topdeckers) or with Duration "at the start of your next turn" bonuses should get a strong positive synergy _and_ a big-move bonus.
   - **Keys on:** the text "extra turn"; "onto your deck"/"on top of your deck"; Duration type.
   - **Evidence:** k2 had 8 and 6 extra turns, Provinces bought on extra turns, a 3-Treasury topdeck loop (T12+/T13 in 2p), and a +9 VP Outpost pair.

3. **An uncontested cheap-gainer Gardens rush means one-sided, low-agency games (lower).**
   - **Rule:** when a "1 VP per N cards in your deck" Victory card coexists with a cheap gainer ("Gain a card costing up to $4") whose gain can take that Victory card, and no strong +Buy or payload alternative is cheap enough to compete, lower the score. Lower it more when Curse-givers are present (they feed the rush) and at higher player counts (more Gardens and more Curses).
   - **Keys on:** Gardens-type text; gainers with a cap ≥ that Victory card's cost; Curse-giving attacks.
   - **Evidence:** in k3-2p and k3-4p the Gardens player won by 14 and 17 VP and led from T6/T10. P1 had $0–2 on most turns, 7 kingdom cards went unbought in 2p, and Witches gave P1 10 Curses in 4p.

4. **"Engine" credit requires real draw (lower).**
   - **Rule:** count Engine as viable only if there is non-terminal draw (+2 Cards or more with +Action, or +1 Card +1 Action with sifting plus a village) _and_ a village reachable at ≤ $4, or a strong terminal draw (+3 Cards or more) with a village. A single terminal +2 Cards (Witch) with Festival at $5 does not count.
   - **Keys on:** "+N Cards" together with "+N Action(s)" text; village cost.
   - **Evidence:** in k3 no engine formed in either game. Throne Room and Festival went unbought and P3 defaulted to Chapel/Big Money.

5. **Cap and throttle long synergy chains (lower).**
   - **Rule:** score a loop by its _limiting resource_. If each iteration costs a Buy and coins (Events, "trash to gain") and gains only one card, scale the weight by the expected number of iterations per turn (about 1–3) instead of summing pairwise steps. Cap total synergy points (for example at 20) and give diminishing returns per extra step.
   - **Keys on:** Event cost, "+Buy" supply, "when you trash this, put it into your hand" style self-recursion.
   - **Evidence:** k1's loop weighed 187 and gave +35, but in play it was one Loot per $2 per Buy. In 2p it fired every other turn and lost to Big Money by 16 VP. In 4p it fired 6 times in total (never more than twice in a turn), and Market Square's Gold reaction never fired.

6. **Discount dominated or orphaned cards from "variety" and "strategies" (lower).**
   - **Rule:** a card that is strictly or practically dominated at its cost tier by another kingdom card (Bandit and Explorer against Witch/Festival/Outpost/Treasury at $5; Harbinger against Pearl Diver at $2–3 for most plans), or whose payoff needs a strategy that cannot form (Feodum without Silver gainers apart from Squire, Tools with no target worth copying), should not add to variety or strategy count.
   - **Keys on:** same-cost comparisons; Alt-VP cards that need a specific gainer.
   - **Evidence:** unbought cards were 3–4 of 10 in k1-2p, 4 of 10 in k1-4p (Sage, Maroon, Tools, Pendant), 5 of 10 in k2-2p, 7 of 10 in k3-2p, and 3 of 10 in k3-4p.

7. **"Big move potential" score (raise).** Detect these from card text:
   - +Buy together with high-ceiling coin payloads ("+$ per differently named Treasure", Pendant-like scaling, Festival plus Gold access). Evidence: k1 T10, $19 and 2 Provinces.
   - Extra turns (k2).
   - One-shot or varied treasure gains (Loot, Spoils, "gain a Loot" Events). Evidence: k1 T5, 3 Loots in a turn, and $12 turns.
   - Gain-several-per-turn effects (gainer + buy, Throne + gainer).
   - Threshold VP (Gardens: the +10/+12/+13 jumps in k3). These are flashy but **only fun if contested**, so pair this with rule 3.
   - Self-topdeck loops (k2 Treasury).

   A kingdom with 2+ of these signals should get a visible bonus.

8. **Player-count-aware attack scoring (adjust both ways).**
   - **Rule:** hand-size attacks (discard down to 3, topdeck a Victory card) stack in 4p into dead turns, so lower the 4p score when ≥2 such attacks exist, especially with small-hand cards (Outpost). Treasure-trashing attacks that pay the attacker (Pirate Ship tokens) become viable and dramatic in 3–4p, so raise them there and lower them in 2p.
   - **Keys on:** "discards down to", "reveals the top 2 cards ... trashes", "Coin token", "draw only 3".
   - **Evidence:** k2-4p had 37 Militia hits and P1's dead turns on T11+, T15+, T16 and T20. Pirate Ship was ignored in 2p but became a viable comeback in 4p (11 treasures trashed).

9. **Attacks that feed the victim, and cross-player triggers.**
   - **Lower:** Curse-givers with Gardens-type cards present, since the curses help the cursed player's deck-size VP (k3-4p).
   - **Raise slightly:** effects that trigger on _anyone's_ gain ("The next time anyone gains a Treasure costing $5 or more") as memorable interaction. In k1-2p it fired on T9. In k1-4p it paid out 4 Loots, 2 of them off opponents' gains.
   - **Keys on:** "anyone gains", "Each other player gains a Curse" combined with "per N cards in your deck".

10. **A shared power is not a combo for one player (lower combo exclusivity, raise accessibility).**
    - **Rule:** if a synergy's core is an Event or Landscape usable by everyone, and setup feeds it to everyone (Shelters' Necropolis plus Peril), treat it as raising the baseline for all strategies, not as a builder's reward. Give a small fun bonus for symmetry and a smaller "combo" bonus.
    - **Evidence:** in k1-2p both players used Peril on Necropolis by T3 and Big Money won. In k1-4p all four players had done it by T3.

11. **Alt-VP or 3-pile rush plus Province players leads to an endgame stall (lower, mostly 3–4p).**
    - **Rule:** when a strong Alt-VP rush is likely (rule 3) and opponents play money, predict penultimate-Province stalls and Duchy/Estate dancing.
    - **Keys on:** Gardens-type text plus cheap gainers at 3+ players.
    - **Evidence:** in k3-4p the last Province went untouched for T20–T23 and P1 had dead turns from T18 to T22.

12. **Villages must be reachable by the strategy that needs them (lower "Villages available").**
    - **Rule:** only give the village bonus if a village costs ≤ $4, or there is draw or economy to reach $5 reliably, and there are terminals that want it.
    - **Evidence:** in k3 Festival at $5 was never bought by the Workshop player, who had 5 terminal collisions. In k2 Festival was irrelevant.
